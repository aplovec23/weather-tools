    4. What the 10 most similar cases did next

    A rough guide only. In a fair test on the 2023–2025 cyclones (each replayed with only its track up to that moment, no forecast track, ten similar cyclones), the position given here was off by about 330 km at 24 hours, 620 km at 48 hours and 890 km at 72 hours. That beats assuming the cyclone does not move (480, 920 and 1,310 km), but it is several times larger than operational forecast errors, and the wind was hardly better than assuming no change. Use it as a comparison with the past, not as forecast guidance.

    5. Intensification outlook

    How did past cyclones in the same place change in strength, every 6 hours over the next two days? The track is ignored here.

    Show the past cyclones used

    Read this as a tendency. Tested on 21,000 past cases, cyclones matched on place, strength and time of year had the same outcome (intensified, steady or weakened) about half the time, against a third by chance; the typical change of the group was off by about 15 km/h, against 21 km/h for assuming no change. Place alone, without strength, tells much less.

    About

    Tracks are compared first, as whole tracks. A past cyclone selected from the record is compared by its complete track with the complete track of every earlier cyclone, over the life of the shorter of the two (how long each cyclone lasted does not count, but one track must be at least half as long as the other); a cyclone that is entered is compared by its positions and forecast track, and a past cyclone matches if it passed along that path. Two things are measured along the tracks, in steps of 50 km: how far apart they lie, and how different their directions of travel are; the time of year counts as well. Tracks whose direction differs by more than the chosen limit on average are left out, which removes those that recurve where the other runs straight. With “Track, then strength”, the closest tracks (as many as were asked for) are then put in order of how alike their wind was along the track. “Strength only” ignores the track and matches the wind over the last four synoptic hours. If only one position is entered there is no track yet, and that position is matched instead. Only cyclones that ended before the date entered are compared, so a past cyclone can be replayed fairly.
